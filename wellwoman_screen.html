About this test

A detailed assessment of female hormonal health

The Female Hormone Screen Gold provides a targeted assessment of several hormones involved in the menstrual cycle, ovarian function and female hormonal health.

The panel measures FSH, LH and oestradiol, alongside testosterone, sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG) and FAI providing more information than a single hormone measurement alone.

Depending on your symptoms and circumstances, these markers may be useful when investigating changes in menstrual cycles, symptoms associated with perimenopause or menopause, fertility concerns or other hormonal symptoms.

What does this test look at?

FSH

Follicle stimulating hormone is involved in ovarian function and the development of ovarian follicles.

Testosterone

Testosterone is present in women in smaller amounts and contributes to libido, muscle function and overall hormonal balance.

Your biomarkers

The Female Hormone Screen Gold includes five hormone markers covering ovarian function, reproductive hormones and androgen status.

FSH
FSH stimulates the growth and development of ovarian follicles that contain eggs. Measuring FSH helps assess ovarian reserve, fertility and menopausal status. Raised FSH levels are commonly seen as women approach the menopause or when ovarian function declines.
LH
LH is responsible for triggering ovulation during the menstrual cycle and plays a central role in female fertility. Measuring LH can help investigate irregular periods, fertility concerns, polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) and menopausal status. LH is usually interpreted alongside FSH and oestradiol.
Oestradiol
Oestradiol is the main form of oestrogen and plays an essential role in regulating the menstrual cycle, fertility, bone strength and reproductive health. Levels naturally fluctuate throughout the menstrual cycle and decline after the menopause. Measuring oestradiol can help assess ovarian function, fertility, menopausal status and certain hormonal disorders. Results should always be interpreted in relation to the stage of the menstrual cycle.
Female Testosterone
Although women produce much lower levels of testosterone than men, it remains important for maintaining muscle strength, bone health, energy levels and libido. Abnormal testosterone levels may be associated with conditions such as polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), adrenal disorders or reduced ovarian function.
SHBG
SHBG regulates the amount of testosterone and oestrogen available to the body's tissues. Changes in SHBG levels can significantly influence hormone balance and may occur with obesity, thyroid disease, liver disease, insulin resistance or certain medications. Measuring SHBG helps improve interpretation of testosterone results.
Free Androgen Index
The Free Androgen Index is a calculated value that estimates how much testosterone is biologically available by comparing total testosterone with SHBG. It provides additional information about androgen status and may help explain symptoms when total testosterone alone does not reflect how much hormone is available to the body's tissues.

Important Pre-Test Information

Menstrual Cycle

FSH, LH and oestradiol can vary significantly during the menstrual cycle. Please tell us the date of the first day of your most recent period so that your results can be interpreted in the appropriate context.

If your cycle is regular, aim for Day 2-5 for hormone tests.

Perimenopause & Menopause

Hormone levels can fluctuate substantially during perimenopause. A single blood test should therefore be interpreted alongside your symptoms, age and menstrual history.

Hormone Treatment & Medication

Please tell us about any hormonal contraception, HRT, fertility treatment or other medicines and supplements you are taking, as these may influence hormone results.
