About this test

A broad look at your abdominal health

The Abdominal Health Screen provides a focused assessment of blood markers associated with the function of several important organs and systems within the body.

The screen includes liver function tests, kidney function and an inflammatory marker, giving you information across several key areas from one blood test.

These results can provide useful information when investigating general wellbeing or symptoms that may be associated with liver, kidney or inflammatory conditions. Results should always be interpreted alongside your symptoms, medical history and clinical assessment.

What's included

Your biomarkers

This screen includes liver function, kidney function and inflammation markers. Explore each group below to learn more about what is measured.

Alanine Transaminase (ALT)
ALT is an enzyme found primarily in liver cells. When liver cells are inflamed or damaged, ALT can be released into the bloodstream. Raised levels can occur in several liver conditions, although results must be interpreted alongside the wider clinical picture.
Aspartate Transaminase (AST)
AST is an enzyme found in the liver as well as in muscle and other tissues. An elevated AST can occur with liver injury but can also be affected by muscle injury, strenuous exercise and other conditions.
Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP)
ALP is an enzyme found mainly in the liver, bile ducts and bones. Raised levels can occur in conditions affecting bile flow or the liver, but can also have an origin outside the liver.
Gamma-Glutamyl Transferase (GGT)
GGT is an enzyme found mainly in the liver and bile ducts. It can become elevated in a number of liver and biliary conditions and may also be influenced by alcohol, medications and other factors.
Bilirubin
Bilirubin is produced when red blood cells are broken down. The liver processes bilirubin before it is excreted in bile. Raised bilirubin can occur for a variety of reasons, including liver or biliary conditions and some inherited conditions such as Gilbert's syndrome.
Albumin
Albumin is a protein made primarily by the liver. It contributes to maintaining fluid balance in the bloodstream and transports a number of substances around the body. Low levels can occur in several conditions, including liver disease, inflammation and some kidney or nutritional disorders.
Total Protein
Total protein measures the combined amount of albumin and globulins in the blood. It can provide information relevant to nutritional status, liver function and other medical conditions.
Globulin
Globulins are a group of proteins involved in immune function and transport of various substances in the blood. Abnormal levels can occur in a range of inflammatory, immune, liver and blood disorders.
Creatinine
Creatinine is a waste product produced by normal muscle metabolism and removed from the blood by the kidneys. It is widely used when assessing kidney function, although muscle mass, hydration and other factors can influence the result.
Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR)
eGFR is an estimate of how effectively the kidneys filter waste products from the blood. It is calculated from creatinine and other factors. A persistently reduced eGFR can indicate impaired kidney function and requires appropriate clinical interpretation.
Urea
Urea is produced when the body breaks down protein and is removed from the bloodstream by the kidneys. It can provide information about kidney function and hydration status.
Sodium
Sodium is an essential electrolyte involved in fluid balance, nerve signalling and muscle function. Abnormal sodium levels can occur with dehydration, kidney conditions, hormonal disorders and some medications.
Chloride
Chloride is an electrolyte that works alongside sodium and other electrolytes to maintain fluid and acid-base balance. Abnormal chloride levels often occur alongside changes in other electrolyte measurements.
High-sensitivity CRP
C-Reactive Protein is produced by the liver in response to inflammation. Levels can rise rapidly following infection, injury or other inflammatory processes. CRP is sensitive but not specific, meaning an elevated result does not identify the exact cause of inflammation.

Important Pre-Test Information

Fasting

Fasting is not routinely required for this screen. You may eat and drink as normal before your appointment unless you have been given different instructions by a healthcare professional.

Medication & Supplements

Please continue your usual medication unless you have been specifically advised otherwise. Tell us about any regular medicines or supplements you take, as these may be relevant when interpreting your results.

Strenuous Exercise

Very strenuous exercise shortly before testing can influence certain blood markers, including some liver-related enzymes. Consider avoiding unusually strenuous exercise immediately before your sample where practical.
