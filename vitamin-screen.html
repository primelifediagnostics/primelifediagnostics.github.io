About this test

A focused check of important vitamins

Vitamins are essential nutrients that help support many of the body's normal functions, including energy metabolism, the immune system, blood formation, nerves and bones.

The Vitamin Screen measures three important nutritional markers: Vitamin D, Vitamin B12 and Folate.

Checking these levels can provide useful information about your current vitamin status, particularly when considered alongside your symptoms, diet and wider health.

What's included

Your biomarkers

The Vitamin Screen includes three nutritional markers. Open each section below to find out more about what is being measured.

Vitamin D
Vitamin D plays a vital role in maintaining healthy bones, teeth and muscles by helping your body absorb calcium and phosphate. It also supports normal immune function and contributes to muscle strength and general wellbeing. Low vitamin D levels are common in the UK and may contribute to tiredness, muscle weakness, bone pain and an increased risk of fractures. Measuring vitamin D helps identify deficiency and monitor response to supplementation.
Vitamin B12
Vitamin B12 is essential for healthy red blood cell production, normal nerve function and DNA synthesis. Low vitamin B12 levels may lead to fatigue, weakness, poor concentration, memory problems, numbness or tingling in the hands and feet, and anaemia. Deficiency may develop due to poor dietary intake, reduced absorption, certain medications or autoimmune conditions such as pernicious anaemia.
Folate
Folate is required for healthy red blood cell production, DNA synthesis and normal cell growth. It works closely with vitamin B12 and is particularly important during pregnancy to support normal fetal development. Low folate levels may contribute to fatigue, anemia and poor cell regeneration. Measuring folate alongside vitamin B12 helps investigate nutritional deficiencies and the causes of macrocytic anemia.

Important Pre-Test Information

Vitamin B12 injections

If you have received a Vitamin B12 injection, the test should ideally be delayed by 7 to 10 days for an accurate baseline assessment.

Active Vitamin B12

We can't provide exact numerical results for active vitamin B12 levels above 150 pmol/L — these results will be displayed as ">150 pmol/L".

Biotin

Please stop taking Biotin supplements 7 days before the test, as they can interfere with certain laboratory results, including thyroid and vitamin B12 tests.


Is this test right for me?

When people choose a Vitamin Screen

This test may be useful if you want to check your current Vitamin D, Vitamin B12 and Folate levels.

It can be considered when investigating symptoms such as persistent tiredness, low energy or reduced wellbeing, particularly where nutritional deficiency may be a consideration.

It may also be useful for people who want to review vitamin levels alongside their diet, lifestyle or existing supplementation.

Blood testing cannot identify every possible cause of symptoms. Results should therefore be interpreted alongside your symptoms and wider medical history.
